# Secondary (Class 10) [toc]

The NIOS Secondary Course (Class 10) offers a flexible and multidisciplinary curriculum that allows learners to choose subjects according to their interests, educational goals, and career aspirations. Unlike conventional schooling systems, NIOS provides greater freedom in subject selection while maintaining a structured syllabus and examination framework.

Learners are generally required to choose a minimum of five subjects for certification, including at least one language subject.

## Subject Selection Rules

For certification, learners should normally:

- Select a minimum of five subjects.
- Choose one or two language subjects from Group A.
- Select the remaining subjects from Group B.
- Additional subjects may be taken as per NIOS rules and prescribed fees.

<Callout type="info">
  A minimum of one and a maximum of two language subjects from Group A are
  permissible for certification. A third language may be taken as an additional
  subject.
</Callout>

## Flexibility in Subject Choice

One of the key strengths of NIOS is its flexible subject selection system. Learners are not restricted to traditional streams and can create combinations based on their individual interests and future goals.

Examples include:

- Mathematics with Psychology and Painting.
- Business Studies with Accountancy and Entrepreneurship.
- Science & Technology with vocational courses.
- Languages combined with skill-based and creative subjects.

This flexibility supports multidisciplinary learning and aligns with the vision of the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020, which encourages learner choice, academic mobility, and skill development.

## Vocational Integration with Academic Subjects

NIOS allows eligible learners to integrate certain vocational courses with academic subjects.

Under this scheme, one vocational course may be taken along with academic subjects and counted as one of the five subjects required for certification.

### Important Rules for Vocational Integration

- One vocational subject may be taken alongside academic subjects.
- The vocational subject is counted as one of the five certification subjects.
- Vocational courses are offered through the same Accredited Institution (AI) selected for academic studies.
- Assessment follows the prescribed NIOS examination and practical scheme.
- Internal assessment may form part of the evaluation process.

<Callout type="warning">
  Learners who choose Data Entry Operations (229) or Entrepreneurship (249) as
  academic subjects are not eligible to opt for an additional vocational subject
  under the integration scheme.
</Callout>

### Vocational Course Assessment

For vocational courses integrated with academic subjects:

- Theory and practical examinations are conducted separately.
- Internal assessment may be applicable depending on the course.
- Learners must satisfy the prescribed passing criteria for certification.
- At present, On-Demand Examinations (ODE) are not available for vocational subjects offered under the integration scheme.

## Scheme of Examination

The examination pattern varies depending on the subject.

### General Pattern

| Category               | Maximum Marks            |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Most Language Subjects | 100                      |
| Mathematics            | 85 Theory + 15 Practical |
| Science & Technology   | 85 Theory + 15 Practical |
| Home Science           | 85 Theory + 15 Practical |
| Painting               | 30 Theory + 70 Practical |
| Data Entry Operations  | 40 Theory + 60 Practical |
| Hindustani Sangeet     | 40 Theory + 60 Practical |
| Carnatic Sangeet       | 40 Theory + 60 Practical |
| Folk Art               | 40 Theory + 60 Practical |

Most academic subjects carry a total of 100 marks, though the distribution between theory and practical components varies depending on the nature of the subject.
