# Practical Records & Files [toc]

Practical records and project files are an important part of many NIOS practical subjects. They document the practical work completed by a learner and may be used during Formative Assessment (FA), practical examinations, viva voce, or other practical evaluations.

Learners should maintain their practical files carefully throughout the course and ensure they are completed before appearing for the practical examination.

## Before Creating Your Practical File

Before starting your practical file, contact your Accredited Institution (AI) or Study Centre whenever possible.

Many study centres conduct practical classes or FA practical sessions where teachers:

- Explain how practical files should be prepared.
- Specify the required format.
- Provide guidance regarding practical examinations.
- Inform learners about submission requirements.

If your study centre provides instructions, always follow their guidance first.

<Callout type="info">
  For information about Formative Assessment (FA), practical classes, and
  practical sessions, please refer to the dedicated FA and Practical Sessions
  pages.
</Callout>

## What Should a Practical File Contain?

The exact requirements vary by subject, but a practical file commonly includes:

1. Cover page with learner details.
2. Subject name and subject code.
3. List of practical activities or experiments.
4. Aim and objectives.
5. Materials or equipment required.
6. Procedure or methodology.
7. Observations.
8. Results or conclusions.
9. Diagrams, screenshots, photographs, or illustrations where applicable.

Some subjects may require additional sections based on the practical manual or instructions from the study centre.

## How Many Experiments Should Be Included?

The exact number may vary depending on the subject and the instructions issued by the study centre.

As a general guideline, learners commonly prepare:

- Approximately 10–12 practical activities or experiments.
- Relevant observations and outcomes for each activity.
- Supporting diagrams, screenshots, or illustrations where applicable.

<Callout type="warning">
  Always follow the latest instructions provided by your study centre or subject
  teacher. Requirements may vary between subjects.
</Callout>

## Subject-Specific Examples

Different subjects require different types of practical records.

### Science Subjects

Subjects such as Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Environmental Science, and Science & Technology may require:

- Experiment records.
- Observations and calculations.
- Diagrams and tables.
- Practical results.

### Computer and Technology Subjects

Subjects such as Computer Science and Data Entry Operations may require:

- Screenshots.
- Software outputs.
- Practical exercises.
- Project-based activities.

### Art and Performance Subjects

Subjects such as Painting, Natyakala, Hindustani Music, Carnatic Music, and Folk Art may require:

- Artwork samples.
- Performance activities.
- Creative portfolios.
- Subject-specific practical records.

### Other Practical Subjects

Subjects such as Geography, Home Science, Physical Education, Early Childhood Care & Education, and Krishi may require:

- Field work reports.
- Activity records.
- Project files.
- Practical assignments.

## Submission of Practical Files

Practical files are generally submitted during or before the practical examination, depending on the instructions issued by the study centre.

Learners should:

- Complete all required practical work.
- Keep records organized and up to date.
- Carry the practical file on the day of the practical examination.
- Follow all instructions issued by the study centre.

<Callout type="warning">
  Do not assume that all study centres follow the same submission procedure.
  Always confirm the process with your Accredited Institution.
</Callout>

## Practical Files and the Practical Examination

Practical files often play an important role in practical examinations.

In many cases, practical examinations may involve:

- Submission of the practical file.
- Written practical questions.
- Questions based on practical activities.
- Questions from the practical manual.
- Viva voce or basic conceptual questions.

Reviewing your practical file before the examination can help you revise practical concepts and procedures effectively.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Leaving experiments incomplete.
- Preparing the file at the last moment.
- Missing observations or results.
- Ignoring instructions issued by the study centre.
- Submitting untidy or poorly maintained records.
- Copying practical work without understanding it.

<Callout type="idea">
  Start preparing your practical file early and update it regularly throughout
  the course. Maintaining the file over time is much easier than completing it
  shortly before the practical examination.
</Callout>
