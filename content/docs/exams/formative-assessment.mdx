# Formative Assessment (FA) [toc]

Formative Assessment (FA) is a practical assessment component used by NIOS for
eligible learners with practical subjects.

Many learners informally refer to Formative Assessment as **5 PCP**,
**5 Mandatory PCP**, or **Practical PCP** because it is conducted through five
mandatory practical assessment sessions. However, the official term used by
NIOS in examination and administrative processes is **Formative Assessment
(FA)**.

<Callout type="warning">
Formative Assessment (FA) contributes 50% of the final practical marks.

The remaining 50% is awarded through the final Practical Examination
(Summative Assessment). Learners should not treat FA as an optional activity,
as poor performance or non-participation can significantly affect the final
practical result.

</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  Formative Assessment (FA) should not be confused with the regular PCP
  (Personal Contact Programme) classes conducted by NIOS Study Centres.

The regular PCP programme consists of academic support and doubt-clearing
sessions, typically spread across approximately 30 days. Formative Assessment
is a separate assessment component that contributes to the final practical
marks of eligible learners.

Some learners and Study Centres may refer to Formative Assessment as:

- 5 PCP
- 5 Mandatory PCP
- Practical PCP
- FA Practical Assessment

However, the official and examination-related term used by NIOS is
**Formative Assessment (FA)**.

</Callout>

### Common Names

Learners may encounter different names for the same assessment.

| Name                      | Usage                                           |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Formative Assessment (FA) | Official NIOS terminology                       |
| 5 PCP                     | Common learner terminology                      |
| 5 Mandatory PCP           | Common learner terminology                      |
| Practical PCP             | Informal terminology used by some Study Centres |
| FA Practical Assessment   | Informal terminology                            |

Although different names may be used, they generally refer to the same
Formative Assessment component.

## Understanding the 5+1 Structure

The practical assessment process is divided into two components.

| Session | Purpose                                            |
| ------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| PCP 1   | Formative Assessment                               |
| PCP 2   | Formative Assessment                               |
| PCP 3   | Formative Assessment                               |
| PCP 4   | Formative Assessment                               |
| PCP 5   | Formative Assessment                               |
| PCP 6   | Summative Assessment (Final Practical Examination) |

<Callout type="idea">
  Learners often refer to this system as the **5+1 System** because five
  practical assessment sessions are followed by one final Practical Examination
  session.
</Callout>

## Marking Scheme

<Callout type="warning">
  Final Practical Marks = 50% Formative Assessment (FA) + 50% Summative
  Assessment (Practical Exam)
</Callout>

| Component                                          | Weightage |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------: |
| Formative Assessment (5 PCP Sessions)              |       50% |
| Summative Assessment (Final Practical Examination) |       50% |

<Callout type="error">
  Missing Formative Assessment sessions may directly impact 50% of the practical
  marks awarded for the subject.
</Callout>

## What Happens If You Miss Formative Assessment?

Missing Formative Assessment can affect your practical marks because FA
contributes **50% of the final practical score**.

<Callout type="warning">
  Formative Assessment carries 50% of the final practical marks.
</Callout>

In practice, the outcome may vary depending on how the Study Centre records
your assessment.

### Case 1: If You Are Marked Present

In many cases, Study Centres award Formative Assessment marks internally and
learners do not receive a separate FA marksheet. As a result, learners usually
do not know their exact FA marks until the final results are declared.

If marks have already been awarded, even if they are low, they generally become
part of the final practical score.

### Case 2: If You Are Marked Absent (AB)

If the Study Centre records your Formative Assessment status as **AB (Absent)**,
NIOS may allow you to appear for the missed Formative Assessment by paying the
prescribed FA examination fee for each affected subject.

<Callout type="info">
  The re-appearance facility is generally available only when the learner has
  been marked absent (AB) in Formative Assessment.
</Callout>

### How to Check Your FA Status?

NIOS provides a Formative Assessment status portal where learners can verify
whether Formative Assessment marks have been submitted.

**FA Status Portal:**

https://exams.nios.ac.in/auth/view-student-formative-assessment

<Callout type="info">
  If the portal displays <strong>"Marks Awarded"</strong>, it generally means
  that Formative Assessment marks have already been submitted for that subject
  and you were not marked absent.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
  The portal only displays the status of Formative Assessment submission. It
  does not show the actual FA marks awarded by the Study Centre.
</Callout>

Learners usually cannot view their individual FA marks separately. The actual
marks become visible only when the final examination results are declared and
the practical marks are published.

| Status Shown  | Meaning                                              |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Marks Awarded | FA marks have been submitted by the Study Centre     |
| AB / Absent   | You have been marked absent for Formative Assessment |

<Callout type="idea">
  If your status shows <strong>AB (Absent)</strong>, you may be eligible to
  re-appear for Formative Assessment by paying the prescribed FA fee for the
  affected subject(s) in the next public examination.

If your status shows <strong>Marks Awarded</strong>, it generally means marks
have already been recorded and the special FA re-appearance facility is not available.

For the latest fee structure, see
[Formative Assessment Fees](/docs/fees/fa-fees).

</Callout>

### Can You Improve Formative Assessment Marks?

Generally, **no**.

If you have already been awarded Formative Assessment marks, even if the marks
are very low, there is usually no separate improvement facility available for
those marks.

The special re-appearance provision is generally meant for learners who were
recorded as **Absent (AB)** and therefore did not receive FA marks.

| Status                    | Re-Appear for FA? |
| ------------------------- | :---------------: |
| Marked AB / Absent        |    ✅ Allowed     |
| Marks Awarded (Any Score) |  ❌ Not Allowed   |

<Callout type="idea">
  Need information about the fees for re-appearing in Formative Assessment? See
  [Formative Assessment Fees](/docs/fees/fa-fees).
</Callout>

## What is Assessed?

Assessment criteria vary by subject, but commonly include:

- Participation during practical sessions
- Practical activities and exercises
- Practical record file maintenance
- Demonstration of practical skills
- Participation with tutors and peers
- Completion of assigned practical tasks

## Assessment Process

<Steps>
  <Step>
    Your Study Centre announces the schedule for the five mandatory Formative
    Assessment sessions.
  </Step>

<Step>
  Attend all scheduled assessment sessions and participate in the prescribed
  practical activities.
</Step>

<Step>
  Maintain practical records, files, journals, or project work as instructed by
  the tutor.
</Step>

<Step>
  Tutors assess your performance throughout the five Formative Assessment
  sessions.
</Step>

<Step>
  Appear for the final Practical Examination (Summative Assessment), which forms
  the remaining 50% of the practical marks.
</Step>

  <Step>
    Final practical marks are calculated using both Formative Assessment and
    Summative Assessment scores.
  </Step>
</Steps>
