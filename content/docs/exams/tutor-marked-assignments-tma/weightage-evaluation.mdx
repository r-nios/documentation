# Weightage & Evaluation [toc]

Tutor Marked Assignments (TMA) form an important part of the NIOS evaluation
system. After submission, TMAs are evaluated by the Study Centre and the marks
are uploaded to NIOS for result processing.

<Callout type="info">
  TMA marks are separate from Theory Examinations, Practical Examinations, and
  Formative Assessment (FA).
</Callout>

## How TMA Evaluation Works

After a learner submits the TMA:

1. The Study Centre evaluates the assignment.
2. Marks are uploaded to the NIOS system.
3. The marks become part of the learner's academic record.
4. NIOS processes the marks according to the applicable TMA weightage for the
   subject.
5. The scaled marks are considered during final result preparation.

## TMA Weightage

TMA contributes **20% of the maximum theory marks** of a subject.

The exact marks available through TMA therefore depend on the theory marks of
the subject.

| Theory Marks | Maximum TMA Marks |
| -----------: | ----------------: |
|          100 |                20 |
|           80 |                16 |
|           70 |                14 |
|           60 |                12 |

<Callout type="info">
  The maximum TMA marks are always calculated as 20% of the theory marks of the
  subject.
</Callout>

### Biology Example

Biology has:

| Component          | Marks |
| ------------------ | ----: |
| Theory Examination |    80 |
| TMA                |    16 |

Since 20% of 80 is 16, the maximum TMA marks that can contribute towards the
final result are **16 marks**.

## Why Do Portal Marks Sometimes Look Different?

Many learners become confused when they see TMA marks on the portal.

The SDMIS evaluation system allows evaluators to award marks out of **20**
regardless of the actual TMA weightage of the subject.

For example, a learner may see:

| Portal Marks | Subject |
| ------------ | ------- |
| 20/20        | Biology |
| 18/20        | Biology |
| 15/20        | Biology |

However, Biology has only **16 obtainable TMA marks** because its theory paper
carries 80 marks.

During result processing, NIOS converts the portal marks according to the
applicable TMA weightage of the subject.

### Example 1

Suppose a learner receives:

| Portal Marks | Maximum |
| ------------ | ------: |
| 20           |      20 |

For Biology:

| Subject | Theory Marks | Obtainable TMA Marks |
| ------- | -----------: | -------------------: |
| Biology |           80 |                   16 |

The full score would therefore be treated as **16/16** during result
processing.

### Example 2

Suppose a learner receives:

| Portal Marks | Maximum |
| ------------ | ------: |
| 18           |      20 |

Biology TMA weightage:

| Calculation  | Result |
| ------------ | -----: |
| 18 ÷ 20 × 16 |   14.4 |

Therefore approximately **14.4 out of 16** would be considered during result
processing.

### Example 3

Suppose a learner receives:

| Portal Marks | Maximum |
| ------------ | ------: |
| 15           |      20 |

Biology TMA weightage:

| Calculation  | Result |
| ------------ | -----: |
| 15 ÷ 20 × 16 |     12 |

Therefore **12 out of 16** would be considered during result processing.

<Callout type="warning">
  The marks displayed by the evaluation portal may not be the final TMA marks
  reflected in the result. NIOS adjusts the marks according to the TMA weightage
  applicable to each subject during result processing.
</Callout>

## What Happens If TMA Is Not Submitted?

| Status             | Outcome                          |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| TMA Submitted      | Marks awarded after evaluation   |
| TMA Not Submitted  | AB (Absent) in TMA component     |
| TMA Submitted Late | Subject to applicable NIOS rules |

<Callout type="warning">
  Although TMA is generally not mandatory for appearing in examinations,
  learners who do not submit TMA may lose marks that could positively impact
  their final result.
</Callout>

## Can I Improve My TMA Marks?

No.

NIOS does not provide a separate TMA improvement facility similar to
Improvement Examinations.

Once TMA marks have been evaluated and uploaded, learners generally cannot
submit another TMA simply to improve their marks.

<Callout type="info">
  Learners should make every effort to submit a complete and well-prepared TMA
  before the submission deadline, as the awarded marks become part of the
  evaluation process.
</Callout>

## Can I View My TMA Marks?

TMA marks are not always available through a dedicated public portal.

Many learners only see the effect of TMA marks when results are declared.

<Callout type="idea">
  If your TMA was not submitted, the TMA component may show
  <strong>AB (Absent)</strong> during result processing.
</Callout>

## Common Questions

### Are TMA Marks Added Separately?

TMA marks are considered as part of the overall evaluation process and are not
treated as a separate examination.

### Is TMA Checked By NIOS?

TMA is normally evaluated by the Study Centre and the awarded marks are then
uploaded to NIOS.

### Why Do I See 20/20 But My Subject Has Only 16 TMA Marks?

This is normal.

Evaluators award marks out of 20 in the portal, but NIOS later converts those
marks according to the TMA weightage of the subject during result processing.

For Biology:

- Portal Marks: 20/20
- Maximum Obtainable TMA Marks: 16/16

Both can be correct at the same time.

## Important Notes

  Keep copies of submitted assignments, screenshots, and submission receipts
  until results are declared.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info">
  TMA marks, Practical Examination marks, Formative Assessment marks, and Theory

</Callout>
